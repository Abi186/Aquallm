# Growth Problems and Shrimp Health

General guidance. For a confirmed disease problem, contact an aquaculture health expert or a laboratory for diagnosis (PCR test).

## Common causes of slow growth

Slow growth (low ADG) usually has more than one cause. Check these in order:

1. Dissolved oxygen is low, especially at night and early morning.
2. Water quality problems: high ammonia, nitrite, unstable pH, or bad pond bottom.
3. Temperature below about 26 degrees Celsius, or a sudden temperature or salinity change.
4. Feeding problems: underfeeding, overfeeding, poor feed quality, or irregular feeding.
5. Stocking density too high for the pond and aeration.
6. Poor seed quality or uneven seed size.
7. Mineral imbalance, especially magnesium, potassium and calcium in low salinity water.
8. Disease or parasites, for example EHP, which causes slow growth with large size variation.
9. Natural slowdown: ADG normally decreases as shrimp become larger and near harvest.

Always compare ADG with the previous sampling and with water-quality records from the same period before deciding on the cause.

## Signs of stress or disease

Shrimp swimming slowly near the pond edge, reduced feed intake, empty gut, soft shell, red body or red gills, white or discoloured faeces, white spots on the shell, uneven size, and running mortality (a few dead shrimp every day). Dead or weak shrimp near the pond edge, or shrimp surfacing at night, can also indicate low oxygen.

## Common shrimp diseases (overview)

- White Spot Syndrome Virus (WSSV): white spots on the shell, red body, sudden high mortality.
- Early Mortality Syndrome / AHPND: mortality in the first month after stocking, pale and shrunken hepatopancreas, empty gut; caused by Vibrio bacteria.
- EHP (Enterocytozoon hepatopenaei): slow growth, size variation, no large sudden mortality.
- White faeces syndrome: white strings floating in the pond or on the check tray, often linked to gut and Vibrio problems.
- Vibriosis: general bacterial infection that increases when water quality and bottom condition are poor.

## What to do when a problem is suspected

Check DO, temperature, pH, ammonia and nitrite immediately. Reduce or stop feeding until the cause is clear. Increase aeration. Collect samples for laboratory or PCR testing. Do not apply random chemicals or medicines. Banned antibiotics and chemicals must never be used; follow the national regulations and your technical advisor.

## Prevention

Use PCR-tested healthy seed, keep good pond preparation and biosecurity, use filtered and treated water, keep stable water quality, avoid overfeeding, maintain good aeration, and keep records for every pond.
