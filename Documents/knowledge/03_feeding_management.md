# Feeding Management

General guidance for vannamei feeding. Always follow the feed company chart and adjust using feed tray observations.

## Feeding principles

Feed is the biggest cost in shrimp farming, so the goal is to give enough feed for good growth without wasting it. Overfeeding pollutes the pond and raises ammonia, nitrite and FCR. Underfeeding slows growth and causes size variation.

## Feed rate

Feed quantity is often calculated as a percentage of shrimp biomass per day. The percentage is high for small shrimp and falls as shrimp grow, from several percent of biomass in the early weeks to around 2 to 3 percent or less near harvest. Use the feed company's chart for the exact number and update it after every ABW sampling.

## Feed tray (check tray) monitoring

Check trays show whether the shrimp ate the feed. If feed is left in the tray, reduce the next feed. If trays are empty quickly, feed can be increased slightly. Poor feed intake can also be an early sign of stress or disease.

## Feeding frequency

Shrimp are fed several times a day, commonly 3 to 5 times, in small portions at regular times. More frequent small feeds improve feed use compared with one or two large feeds.

## When to reduce or stop feeding

Reduce feed when DO is low, after heavy rain or sudden weather change, during moulting stress, when check trays show leftover feed, when ammonia or nitrite are high, and when disease is suspected. Skipping a feed during low DO protects the pond.

## Feed quality and storage

Use feed from a trusted brand, store it in a cool dry place, and do not use feed that is mouldy or old. Poor feed raises FCR and can cause gut and growth problems.

## Reading FCR

FCR above about 1.8 means too much feed is being used for each kg of shrimp produced. Check for overfeeding, low survival (fewer shrimp than estimated), poor water quality, disease, and sampling errors in ABW.
